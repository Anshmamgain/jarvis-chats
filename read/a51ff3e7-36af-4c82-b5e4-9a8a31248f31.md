**The sections:**

1. **What you do** (6 Qs) — same thing explained to a 12-year-old vs. a peer. The gap between the two is their register range.
2. **Opinions** (6 Qs) — "what does your industry repeat that actively hurts people?" Let them rant. Rants are the purest voice sample you'll get.
3. **How you talk** (8 Qs) — last five unedited Slack messages; words they'd notice missing; words that make them cringe. Then hand them a generic ghostwritten post and ask which exact line they'd never say.
4. **Stories** (5 Qs) — told the way they'd tell it at a bar, not the LinkedIn version.
5. **Do nots** (5 Qs) — including "what would make you text me at 2am saying take that down."
6. **Proof** (4 Qs) — every number they can defend, public vs. private.
7. **Deep cut** (5 Qs) — Ruben's "one piece of writing you're proud of" lives here.

**Two things that matter more than the questions:**

Record and transcribe. The transcript is the asset — voice comes from how they talk, not what they claim about themselves. Extract real phrases into `voice.md`, never invent.

Then ship 3 sample posts in 48h and ask one thing: "which line would you never say?" Their edits are the highest-signal voice data in the whole process.
