AI-written, sir. High confidence — 90%+.

**The three that convict it:**

1. **"I have not found anyone credible who teaches this, so treat it as just what I do."** This is a fact-check hedge that leaked into the copy. No human writes a sourcing disclaimer about their own pricing habit. That's a model obeying a "don't make unverifiable claims" instruction.

2. **The Hormozi block.** Name-dropping him is human. Taxonomising his three guarantee types and then mapping your own onto it ("ours is the first") is retrieval output pasted mid-post. The POV snaps from *me* to *book summary* and back.

3. **The comma-conjunction fingerprint.** "I price at or under it, so nobody has to invent a budget." / "She tells her boss it works, and that closes the deal." / "We give the money back, and we almost never have to." Same clause shape four times. That's a model following a "use conjunctions, no staccato" rule — ironically, our own house rule.

**Supporting:** perfectly balanced 2-3 sub-bullets per section, "Y Combinator taught me..." bolted on with no link to anything above, "Nobody warned me that..." retrospective-wisdom close, and a repost-bait CTA.

**What's actually good:** the opening line and the 11pm nurse. Either a human wrote the hook and let a model fill the body, or it was prompted well. But the nurse drifts — singular, then "our nurses," then "she." Human writers don't lose their own example.

AI-drafted, sir — but a better prompt and a heavier human edit than the last one. ~70-75%, not 90.

**The tell that does it:**

The hook promises non-dilutive **cash** — "$305k and take nothing." Then the list totals NSF/NIH grants with Google, AWS, Microsoft and Anthropic **cloud credits** into one "$1.3 million." Credits aren't money. You can't make payroll with AWS credits, and any founder who's actually filed an SBIR feels that distinction in their bones. That's not a style slip — it's a model adding up a list it assembled without understanding what's in it. The arithmetic is right (1,353) and the meaning is wrong. Very characteristic.

**Second:** the "~" separators. Natural form is "NSF SBIR — Up to $305,000." Swapping in a tilde makes it read "approximately up to," which is nonsense. That's an em-dash-stripping pass — an anti-detection step that is itself a detection signal. Same with the lone "&" among otherwise spelled-out "and."

**Third:** three different diagnoses in five lines. Nobody applies because the odds look bad → no, because the forms are boring → no, because nobody knows it exists. A human with a real thesis picks one and drives it.

**What argues human:** "You cannot post 'thrilled to announce' about a government grant" is a genuinely good, LinkedIn-native joke. Contractions survive. The closer is punchy. Someone with taste worked on this.
