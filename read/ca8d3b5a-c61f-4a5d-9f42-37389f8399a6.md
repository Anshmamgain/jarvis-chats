### Alfred  
Sir, the moves I stole, so you can reuse them:

1. **Flat observation, first person, no hook theatrics.** "I read a lot of what founders write." PG never announces that an insight is coming.
2. **Kill the obvious explanation fast.** "At first I assumed this was luck. It isn't." That is his standard second beat.
3. **Reduce to two causes.** He does this constantly (superlinear returns reduces to exponentials and thresholds). Two is the number that reads as thinking rather than listing.
4. **Import a mechanism from another domain.** The thresholds paragraph is lifted structurally from Superlinear Returns, applied to attention.
5. **Strongest claim that stays true, qualified with one word.** "usually," "almost everything." Not "in my experience, it could be argued."
6. **End small and flat.** No CTA, no "thoughts?". The last line is 8 words and does the work.

One honest note: no PG essay has a hook, and LinkedIn's first 2 lines gate everything. This opener survives because the observation is itself the anomaly. If it underperforms, the fix is not adding a hook, it is finding a sharper anomaly.
